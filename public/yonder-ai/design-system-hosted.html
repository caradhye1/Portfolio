Yonder AI Design System

The system behind Yonder AI, a social intelligence product that tells brand analysts who is driving a narrative, whether to care, why it is happening and what to do. One stylesheet holds every token and component on this page, and every example here is live.

Principles

Purple acts, ink selects

Purple means “you can do this”. Ink (near-black) means “this is on”. Tabs, chips and checkboxes never compete with the primary action.

Three hues are reserved

Red, amber and blue mean High, Moderate and Low impact and nothing else. Destructive actions are ink. Charts use a separate palette.

Contrast is measured

Every text pair is computed on this page from the live tokens. Nothing is quoted from memory, and nothing ships below WCAG AA.

Meaning never rides on colour alone

Impact carries a level glyph and a word. Selection changes fill and weight. Errors carry an icon and a sentence.

Every state is designed

Hover, focus, pressed, disabled, loading, empty and error exist for every component before it is used in a screen.

Tokens in three tiers

Primitives hold raw values. Semantic roles say what a value is for. Components only ever reference roles.

Spacing and radius

A 4px base with a 2px half step. Radius grows with the size of the container: tags and chips are pills, controls are small, cards are large, the hero and modals are extra large.

TokenValueScale

Layout and app shell

Every page sits in the same shell: a fixed top bar, a floating side navigation, and one content column on the gradient. Column widths are tokens, so a page picks a width by its job: reading, comparing or editing.

Top bar and side navigation

The top bar carries the wordmark and account actions only. The side navigation carries the brand being tracked first, as a letter avatar, then the five product areas. The current area fills purple, because navigating is an action, not a selection.

TokenValueUse

Elevation

Shadows carry a violet undertone, never gray. Three levels: resting cards, raised or hovered surfaces, overlays. A surface only rises when it can be acted on or sits above the page.

Motion

Three durations and one standard curve. Only transform and opacity animate. With reduced motion the state still changes; the travel is dropped.

--lift moves a hovered card up by 1px. --press scales a pressed control to 98%. Both are transforms, so nothing around them reflows.

Part 2

Iconography

Icon set

One set, drawn on a 24px grid with a 1.75 stroke, round caps and round joins. No filled icons except a pressed star. Every icon below is the exact symbol the product uses.

The grid

  • 24 × 24 canvas with 2px of padding on every side.
  • 1.75 stroke at every size. Icons scale; strokes do not get heavier.
  • Round caps and joins, to match Heebo’s rounded terminals.
  • Shapes are drawn from simple primitives so the set reads as one family.

Using icons

Sizes

16 · inline, small buttons20 · default, navigation24 · wordmark, stat cards

Icons with words, icons alone

Do

  • Lead a button label with an icon only when it speeds recognition.
  • Give every icon-only button an accessible name and a tooltip.
  • Use the trend icons with a word: Rising, Steady, Falling.

Don’t

  • Use emoji, or icons from another set.
  • Colour an icon red, amber or blue unless it shows an impact level.
  • Use an icon as the only signal of an error or a state.

Data visualisation

Charts are inline SVG coloured by class. Faction series use the categorical palette in fixed order; the narrative total is purple; benchmarks are neutral and dashed. Every chart has a legend, a keyboard-readable tooltip and a text summary. Impact scales may use the reserved hues; nothing else may.

Areas fade from their hue at --opacity-viz-area down to --opacity-viz-area-floor at their base, with a solid 2px edge on top so the value still reads clearly. The fade only changes opacity, never hue.

Modals trap focus, close on Escape and on the scrim, and return focus to the control that opened them. Popovers close on Escape and outside click. Side panels slide in from the left for curation. Toasts confirm and offer undo; they never carry the only copy of an error.

Part 4

Pages

How the components compose into each screen of the product. Select a screen to open it in the prototype.

Daily Digest

  1. Toolbar. Curate, filter and sort as raised icon buttons, with a filter count. .icon-btn--raised
  2. Date pager. Steps through days; the next arrow disables on today. .date-pager
  3. Digest summary. Title, date and the flagged narratives as links, on one raised card.
  4. Result count. “Showing 6 of 6 narratives”, announced when filters change.
  5. Narrative cards. In curated order, flagged first. .narrative-card
  6. Column. Reading width, --size-content.

Narrative Analysis

  1. Large modal. Keeps the digest behind it, so returning is one Escape away. .modal
  2. Four question tabs. Who, care, why, what, in that order. .tabs
  3. Chart. Stacked faction areas with a scrubbable, keyboard-readable tooltip. .chart
  4. Legend as control. Hide a faction and the rest re-stack. .legend__item
  5. What this means. A plain-language reading of the chart beside it.
  6. Add a note. A tonal action; saved notes become callouts. .btn--tonal

Factions

  1. Page header. Title, one-line description and a search field. .page-header
  2. Faction cards. Avatar, name, stance badge and how many of today’s narratives it joins. The whole card is the link.
  3. Grid. Three columns at comparing width, --size-content-wide.

Faction profile

  1. Back link. Returns to the Factions list. .back-link
  2. Identity. Display name, stance badge and a lead paragraph. .text-display .text-lead
  3. Stat grid. Six columns; cards span two or three. .stat-card
  4. Encodings. Wedge, megaphones, alignment and meters, each with an info tooltip.
  5. Aligned factions. Links to related profiles.

Convos

  1. Page header. Title, description and the page’s one primary action.
  2. Conversation card. Name, status badge, description and a metadata line of groups and dates.

Edit a conversation

  1. Back link and title. Back, then “Edit a Conversation”.
  2. Fields. Name, start date with its allowed range as a hint, description. .field
  3. Query group. Field select, match select, keyword tags, “and not” tags. .tag-input
  4. Conditions. Add or remove “and any of” rows; groups join with OR.
  5. Read-back. The query restated as a sentence, so mistakes surface before saving.

Curate panel

  1. Side panel. Slides from the left; the digest stays visible. .panel
  2. Step indicator. Two bars beside the title.
  3. Narrative rows. Impact mark, title, move up, move down, remove. .list-row
  4. Footer. One full-width primary action per step.

Alerts

  1. Page header. Search and Add alert.
  2. Table in a card. Title as the link, row menu for secondary actions. .table
  3. Empty values. “Any faction” in tertiary text, never a blank cell.
  4. Footer. “Showing 1 to 5 of 7 alerts” and pagination.

Part 5

Interactions

The patterns that make the product feel fast. Each one below is live: use it with a mouse, then with the keyboard.

Instant filters

    Why it works this way

    • Filtering is exploring. Each chip applies the moment it is touched; there is no Apply button.
    • The count updates and is announced to screen readers.
    • No chip on means everything shows. Nothing matching shows an empty state with one way out.

    Collapse and star

    Moderate impactSteady
    Workers deserve a living wage. #FightFor15
    Inauthenticity9%

    Why it works this way

    • Triage is a pass through a list. A collapsed card keeps its title and impact, so the page shrinks as the work gets done.
    • The toggle reports aria-expanded and its label and icon change with it.
    • Star is a toggle button: pressed fills purple, and a toast confirms.

    Remove, undo, reorder

    Narratives

      Why it works this way

      • Undo, not “Are you sure?”. Removal is instant; the toast offers Undo and puts the row back where it was.
      • Order is editorial, so it gets explicit up and down buttons instead of drag only.
      • Focus follows the row you moved, and the new position is announced.
      • The first row cannot move up and the last cannot move down; those buttons disable.

      Keyword tags

      Keywords
      Press Enter or comma to add. Backspace removes the last one.

      Why it works this way

      • Keywords are objects, not a string. Nobody has to get separators or quoting right.
      • Duplicates are ignored; each tag has its own named remove button.
      • The query reads itself back as a sentence while you type.

      Modal focus

      Open it, press Tab past the last button, then Escape.

      Why it works this way

      • Focus moves into the modal on open and cannot leave it while it is open.
      • Escape, the close button and the scrim all close it. Focus returns to the button that opened it.
      • Sending with an empty message shows the error inline, not in a toast.

      Part 6

      Using the system

      Content and voice

      The interface talks like a sharp analyst briefing a colleague: plain, specific and short. Labels are the questions people already have in their heads.

      Do

      • “Should I care?” as a tab label.
      • “21% inauthentic, against a typical 20%.” A number with its benchmark.
      • “Give this conversation a name.” An error that says how to fix it.
      • “Query group deleted. Undo.” Past tense, with a way back.
      • Sentence case everywhere, except overlines.

      Don’t

      • “Authenticity metrics” as a tab label.
      • “21%” on its own.
      • “Invalid input.”
      • “Are you sure you want to delete?”
      • Title Case Buttons Or Exclamation Marks!

      Accessibility

      Contrast

      Every text pair meets WCAG AA at 4.5:1, every control outline and graphic at 3:1. Measured live under Contrast.

      Focus

      A 2px purple outline with a 2px offset on every interactive element. It is never removed, only restyled.

      Not colour alone

      Impact has a glyph and a word; selection changes fill and weight; errors have an icon and a sentence.

      Names and roles

      Icon buttons are named. Toggles use aria-pressed, disclosures aria-expanded, tabs a real tablist.

      Announcements

      Result counts, moves and toasts sit in polite live regions, so changes are heard as well as seen.

      Reduced motion

      With reduced motion on, states still change but nothing travels.

      Rules

      • Use a semantic role. If no role fits, propose one; do not reach for a primitive or a literal.
      • Keep red, amber and blue for impact level. A delete button is ink.
      • One primary button per view.
      • Never set text lighter than --color-text-tertiary or smaller than 12px.
      • Every interactive element has hover, focus-visible, pressed and disabled states, and a 2px focus outline that is never removed.
      • Animate transform and opacity only, and honour reduced motion.
      • Pair every colour signal with a word, an icon or a shape.
      • Do not nest cards. Do not use the gradient behind text blocks; it is the app canvas.

      Scope: the product is designed for desktop, 1280px and wider.
